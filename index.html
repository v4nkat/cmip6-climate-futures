03 / Reading the chart

The near future is shared.
The late century is chosen.

A

Near-term inertia

Through the 2030s, the scenario medians remain close. Past emissions and the climate system’s delayed response make some additional warming common to every pathway.

B

A widening fork

After mid-century, pathways separate rapidly. Under SSP1-1.9 the ensemble median levels off; under SSP5-8.5 it continues climbing.

C

Uncertainty is not ignorance

Models disagree on the exact amount of warming, but by late century the differences between scenarios are larger than much of the within-scenario model spread.

04 / Method & data

From 44 models to a legible range

The source archive contains global-mean near-surface air temperature (tas) anomalies from CMIP6 models. Values are area-weighted, aggregated to annual means, referenced to 1850–1900, and then averaged into multi-year periods by the archive maintainers.

This visualization keeps the nine non-overlapping decades from 2011–2020 through 2091–2100. Within every scenario and decade, it computes the median and the 10th/90th percentiles across one ensemble member per model. No models or outliers are removed.

05 / Design write-up

Why this design

Visual encodings

Position on a common vertical scale encodes temperature because it supports precise comparison. Scenario hue distinguishes pathways, while line weight and opacity establish focus. A continuous line emphasizes trajectory; the percentile ribbon makes model disagreement visible without obscuring the median. The detail view changes the unit of analysis from an aggregate to individual models, exposing the distribution behind each ribbon.

Interaction rationale

Scenario toggles support direct comparison without forcing all five pathways on every reader. Hovering provides details on demand; the decade slider links the trajectory view to a model-level dot plot. This coordinated interaction answers two related questions: how futures change over time, and how much models agree at a chosen moment.

Alternatives considered

A choropleth was rejected because the available question concerns global scenario divergence rather than local geography. Small multiples reduced overlap but made cross-scenario comparison harder. A single mean line hid uncertainty, while a min–max band overemphasized extreme models; the median with a 10th–90th percentile interval was more robust and legible.

Development process

The work proceeded through data inspection, question scoping, paper sketches, implementation, and responsive testing. Data transformation and the coordinated D3 views required the most effort, followed by interaction polish and accessible labeling.
